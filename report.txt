Women show a significantly higher survival rate than men, indicating that gender played a major role in rescue priority.
Children also have a noticeably better chance of survival compared to adult men, though their advantage is smaller than that of women.
While not every female or child survived—and some adult men in higher classes did—the numbers clearly lean toward preferential treatment for women and younger passengers during evacuation.
 
The data strongly supports the “wealth” hypothesis.
Both Method A and Method B show that passengers in higher classes or higher fare groups had better survival rates.
Even though the methods group the data differently, they lead to the same conclusion: wealth increased the chances of survival.
